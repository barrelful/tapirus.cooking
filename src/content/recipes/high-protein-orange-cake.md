An orange-forward single-layer cake using blended orange flesh and fine zest for clean citrus flavor without bitterness, finished with a thin setting glaze.  
Protein is increased with fat-free Greek yogurt, egg whites, and unflavoured whey isolate while sugar and fat are reduced.  
Designed for 10 repeatable wedges from a 25 cm round pan, with stable refrigerator storage and optional freezing.

- ⏲️ Prep time: ~25 min  
- 🍳 Cook time: ~35–40 min  
- 🍽️ Servings: 10 portions (about 120 g each)  
- ❄️ Freezer-friendly (individual wedges)

## Ingredients

Orange cake batter (25 cm round pan)
- 2 medium oranges (about 250–270 g total), prepped as described in Directions
- Zest of 1 orange (finely grated, no white pith)
- 200 g all-purpose flour
- 20 g cornstarch
- 40 g unflavoured whey protein isolate
- 10 g baking powder
- 1.5 g baking soda
- 2 g fine salt
- 100 g fat-free Greek yogurt
- 50 g neutral oil
- 60 g unsweetened applesauce
- 95 g granulated sugar
- 35 g erythritol
- Sucralose equivalent to 25–30 g sugar sweetness (per product label)
- 100 g whole eggs (about 2 large)
- 100 g egg whites
- 5 mL vanilla extract
- 1–2 g glycerol monostearate (GMS), optional
- 5–10 g orange juice, only if needed to help blending
- Up to 10 g extra flour, only if batter is too loose after resting

Thin orange glaze
- 50 g icing sugar
- 20–25 g powdered erythritol
- 15–18 mL orange juice
- Small pinch fine salt
- 2–5 mL hot water, only if needed to loosen

## Directions

**Orange cake batter**
1. Heat the oven to 170 C conventional. Grease a 25 cm round pan, line the base with parchment, and lightly grease the parchment.
2. Prep the oranges: wash and dry well. Finely zest one orange, avoiding white pith. Peel both oranges completely, removing seeds, central core, and as much white pith as practical. Cut into chunks.
3. Whisk the flour, cornstarch, whey isolate, baking powder, baking soda, and salt in a bowl until evenly combined.
4. Add the orange flesh, zest, Greek yogurt, oil, applesauce, sugar, erythritol, sucralose, eggs, egg whites, vanilla, and optional GMS to a blender.
5. Blend until fully smooth, about 30–45 seconds. Add 5–10 g orange juice only if needed to help the blender run cleanly.
6. Pour the blended wet base into the dry mix and fold just until no dry pockets remain. Do not overmix.
7. Rest the batter for 8–10 minutes so the whey and flour hydrate, then fold once more gently.
8. Check batter consistency: it should fall in thick ribbons and level slowly in about 10–14 seconds. If too loose, fold in flour 5 g at a time (up to 10 g).
9. Transfer the batter to the pan and level it.
10. Bake for 34–40 minutes, until the center springs back lightly, a skewer comes out with moist crumbs, and the internal temperature is about 96–98 C.
11. Cool in the pan for 15 minutes, then unmold onto a rack and cool completely before glazing.

**Thin orange glaze**
1. Whisk the icing sugar, powdered erythritol, orange juice, and salt until smooth.
2. If needed, add a small amount of hot water to reach a thin, pourable consistency.
3. Spoon the glaze over the cooled cake and spread into a thin, even layer. Leave to set for 20–30 minutes before slicing.

## Portioning & Storage

**Per portion (plated):**
- 1 portion = 1/10 cake, about 120 g finished cake

**Batch yield:**
- Total finished yield: about 1,150–1,200 g

**Storage**
- Refrigerator: up to 4 days, covered
- Freezer: up to 1 month in individually wrapped wedges
- Thaw frozen wedges overnight in the refrigerator or 1–2 hours at cool room temperature
- For best texture, serve cold or let refrigerated wedges stand 15–20 minutes before eating

## Notes

- Orange flavor comes from blended peeled fruit plus fine zest, not peel chunks, reducing bitterness risk while keeping a strong citrus profile.
- Removing as much white pith (albedo) as possible is critical; it is the main source of bitterness.
- Blending time is kept short (30–45 s) to avoid over-extracting peel oils and incorporating excess air.
- Cornstarch improves crumb softness and offsets firmness from whey and reduced sugar.
- Whey isolate is limited to 40 g to balance protein increase with good cake texture.
- A small amount of baking soda complements baking powder by neutralizing acidity from the oranges, improving rise and color.
- Applesauce replaces part of the fat and helps maintain moisture during refrigerated storage.
- Optional GMS improves emulsion stability and helps maintain a softer crumb over time.
- The glaze intentionally keeps a higher proportion of real sugar for better shine, smoother texture, and cleaner set.
