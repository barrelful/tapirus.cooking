Yeasted laminated cheddar biscuits designed to keep the flaky-roll hybrid texture of the original style.  
Uses half-fat cheddar, less butter in the dough, and a smaller sugar load to reduce calories without stripping the butter-laminated structure.  
Design intent: 12 repeatable freezer-capable biscuits with clean square cuts, stable proofing, and reliable reheating.

- ⏲️ Prep time: ~45 min active
- 🍳 Cook time: ~15–20 min
- 🍽️ Servings: 12 biscuits (about 75 g each baked)
- ❄️ Freezer-friendly (baked biscuits)

## Ingredients

Dough
- 380 g all-purpose flour, plus **10–20 g** extra for dusting
- 25 g skim milk powder
- 160 g half-fat mature cheddar, grated on the large holes of a box grater
- 12 g sugar
- 7 g fine salt
- 4.5 g instant yeast
- 30 g unsalted butter, cold, cut into small pieces
- 260 g low-fat buttermilk, at room temperature
- 60 g fat-free Greek yogurt, at room temperature

Seasoned lamination butter
- 70 g unsalted butter, cool room temperature
- 4 g garlic, finely grated or minced
- 2 g dried dill
- 2 g onion powder
- 0.5 g cayenne pepper
- 0.5 g finely ground black pepper
- 1 g fine salt

To finish
- 15 g buttermilk for brushing

## Directions

**Dough**
1. Combine the flour, skim milk powder, sugar, salt, and instant yeast in a large bowl.
2. Add the **30 g cold butter** and rub it into the dry ingredients with your fingertips until no large pieces remain; the largest pieces should be roughly lentil-sized.
3. Add the grated cheddar and toss to distribute it evenly.
4. Whisk the buttermilk and Greek yogurt together until smooth.
5. Pour the liquid into the bowl and mix with a spatula or hand mixer on low just until a shaggy dough forms.
6. Turn the dough onto the work surface and knead gently **1–2 minutes** only until the dry patches are absorbed and the dough is mostly uniform.
7. Form the dough into a ball, return it to the bowl, cover, and let it rise at room temperature until **50–75% expanded**, about **45–75 minutes**.

**Seasoned lamination butter**
1. Mix the **70 g butter**, garlic, dill, onion powder, cayenne, black pepper, and **1 g salt** until completely homogeneous and spreadable.

**Lamination**
1. Turn the risen dough onto a lightly floured surface and press it into a rectangle.
2. Roll the dough to a rectangle about **8–10 mm thick**.
3. Spread the seasoned butter evenly over the lower **two-thirds** of the dough, leaving the upper third unbuttered.
4. Fold the unbuttered top third down over the middle third, then fold the bottom third up over it to make a letter fold.
5. Rotate the dough **90 degrees** so the seam is on the right.
6. Roll again to about **8–10 mm thick** and repeat the same letter fold.
7. Wrap the dough and chill it in the freezer for **10 minutes**, then refrigerate for **30 minutes**.
8. Roll the chilled dough a final time to about **8–10 mm thick**, then complete one more letter fold.
9. Flatten the packet lightly so it is no more than about **4 cm thick**, wrap it, and refrigerate for at least **6 hours** and up to **16 hours**.

**Cut, proof, and bake**
1. Heat the oven to **200 °C** and line a sheet pan with parchment paper.
2. Trim the outer edges of the chilled dough lightly to expose clean layers.
3. Cut the dough into **12 equal biscuits** by dividing it into **3 rows by 4 columns**.
4. Arrange the biscuits on the lined tray with space between them.
5. Cover loosely and proof in a cool room-temperature spot until slightly domed and visibly aerated, about **60–90 minutes**.
6. Brush the tops lightly with **15 g buttermilk**.
7. Bake on the centre rack for **15–20 minutes**, rotating once if needed, until deeply golden on top and browned on the base.
8. Cool on the tray for **5 minutes**, then transfer to a rack.

## Portioning & Storage

**Per portion (plated):**
- 1 portion = **1 biscuit**, about **75 g baked**.

**Storage**
- Refrigerator: up to **4 days** in a covered container.
- Freezer: freeze baked biscuits individually, then store airtight for up to **2 months**.
- Reheat from refrigerated: **180 °C for 6–8 minutes**.
- Reheat from frozen: **180 °C for 10–12 minutes**.

## Notes

- Half-fat mature cheddar reduces fat relative to full-fat cheddar while preserving strong flavour; using a mature cheese is important because the quantity is lower than the source recipe.
- Butter is kept primarily in the laminated layer, where it contributes the most to flake definition; the dough itself uses less butter than the original style.
- A small amount of skim milk powder improves protein density and browning without making the dough behave like a whey-enriched bread dough.
- The long refrigerated rest improves layer definition, makes square cutting cleaner, and keeps the final proof more controlled.
- These biscuits are best reheated in dry oven heat rather than a microwave; microwave reheating softens the laminated structure.
