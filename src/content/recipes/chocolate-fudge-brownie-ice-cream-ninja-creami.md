Dense chocolate ice cream with a cooked egg-yolk base and frozen malted brownie pieces.\
Retains double cream, sugar and honey for richness, with milk powder and a small amount of whey for body.\
Designed for one Ninja Creami pint divided into two weighed portions after mixing in the brownie.

- ⏲️ Prep time: ~20 min active, plus at least 4 hours chilling and 24 hours freezing
- 🍳 Cook time: ~10–15 min
- 🍽️ Servings: 2 portions (approximately 248 g each before cooking losses), from one pint
- Freezer-friendly: freeze the base level in the Creami pint before processing

## Ingredients

**Chocolate ice cream base**
- 225 g semi-skimmed milk
- 70 g double cream
- 45 g granulated sugar
- 15 g mild honey
- 5 g erythritol
- 26 g cocoa powder
- 20 g skimmed milk powder
- 10 g chocolate whey protein powder
- 1 large egg yolk (approximately 18 g)
- 2 g vanilla extract
- 0.6 g fine salt (adjust to taste only in a subsequent batch; use 0.6 g initially)
- 0.30 g xanthan gum

**Mix-in**
- 60 g [freezer-friendly malted brownie](/recipe/freezer-friendly-malted-brownies/), cut into 8–10 mm cubes and frozen

## Directions

**Equipment and dry mixture**
1. Gather a Ninja Creami and its compatible pint, a saucepan, a mixing bowl, a whisk, a spatula, a probe thermometer, kitchen scales and a 0.01 g-resolution scale for the xanthan gum; set out an immersion blender if available.
2. Prepare a larger bowl for an ice-water bath and ensure the freezer has a level shelf for the pint.
3. Combine the sugar, erythritol, cocoa powder, skimmed milk powder and xanthan gum in the dry mixing bowl.
4. Mix very thoroughly until the xanthan gum is evenly distributed throughout the dry ingredients.

**Cooked chocolate base**
1. Put the milk, double cream and honey into the saucepan.
2. Heat gently to **40–50°C**, stirring to dissolve the honey.
3. Gradually whisk or immersion-blend in the dry mixture until completely smooth, with no cocoa or milk-powder lumps; keep the blender head submerged to minimise foam.
4. Add the egg yolk and mix thoroughly.
5. Heat gently while stirring continuously and scraping the bottom until the mixture reaches approximately **82°C** throughout; do not boil.
6. Remove the saucepan from the heat immediately.
7. Stir occasionally while the base cools to **55–60°C**.
8. Add the chocolate whey protein, vanilla extract and salt.
9. Blend briefly until completely homogeneous, or whisk thoroughly if no blender is available.

**Cooling and freezing**
1. Transfer the base into a clean heatproof container and stand it in an ice-water bath, keeping bath water out of the base.
2. Stir the base periodically with a clean utensil to cool it rapidly; transfer it to the refrigerator promptly and chill to **4°C or below within 2 hours** of finishing cooking.
3. Cover and refrigerate for **at least 4 hours**, preferably overnight.
4. Stir the cold base and pour it into the compatible Creami pint, staying below its **maximum fill line**; do not force excess base into the container.
5. Fit the storage lid and freeze upright on a level shelf for **at least 24 hours**, until fully frozen with a level surface.

**Spinning and brownie mix-in**
1. Install the fully frozen pint according to the machine's instructions and run **Ice Cream**.
2. If the first spin is powdery or crumbly, run **Re-Spin** before adding the brownie; do not automatically add milk.
3. Make a hole down through the centre of the processed ice cream, following the model's mix-in instructions.
4. Add **60 g frozen brownie cubes**, keeping within the model's fill and inclusion limits.
5. Run **Mix-In**.
6. Let the finished ice cream stand for **2–3 minutes** before serving.
7. Weigh the finished ice cream and divide it into two equal portions.

## Portioning & Storage

**Per portion (ready to eat):**
- Half the finished pint: approximately 248 g, comprising half the cooked chocolate base and approximately 30 g brownie.
- Ingredient mass before cooking is approximately 436.9 g base plus 60 g brownie, or 496.9 g total; evaporation and transfer losses reduce the finished mass.
- Weigh the actual finished batch after Mix-In and divide that weight by two for an exact serving mass.

**Storage**
- Refrigerator (cooked, unprocessed base): keep covered at 4°C or below for up to 2 days after cooking; the overnight maturation is included in that time.
- Freezer (unspun base): keep covered in the compatible pint at −18°C or below for up to 1 month for best quality; freeze level for at least 24 hours before the first spin.
- Freezer (finished ice cream): transfer individual half-pint portions into airtight freezer-safe containers and freeze for up to 2 weeks for best quality.
- Frozen leftovers: temper an individual portion in the refrigerator for 10–15 minutes, checking until scoopable. Do not process loose frozen portions or an uneven, partly filled block in the Creami unless the model's manual explicitly permits it.
- Serving: eat promptly after processing; do not repeatedly thaw and refreeze. Reheating is not appropriate for this dish.

## Notes

- All supplied ingredient quantities are retained. This is a richness-first formulation, not a reduced-calorie or high-protein ice cream; the whey supplies a small amount of additional protein and milk solids rather than defining the recipe's nutritional direction.
- Double cream provides richness and fat, cocoa provides concentrated chocolate flavour, and skimmed milk powder increases body through protein and lactose. The yolk contributes emulsifying components and a cooked custard structure.
- Honey and erythritol contribute sweetness and freezing-point depression. Their effect depends on the whole formulation; they do not guarantee that refrozen ice cream remains scoopable straight from the freezer.
- Xanthan is approximately 0.07% of the base mass. Thorough dry dispersion reduces clumping; avoid increasing the amount casually because excess gum can produce a sticky texture.
- A fine-resolution scale is needed to measure 0.30 g xanthan consistently. A teaspoon estimate is not an equivalent replacement; an ordinary whole-gram kitchen scale is suitable for the larger ingredients but not this dose.
- A probe thermometer makes the cooked-yolk endpoint repeatable. Visual thickening alone does not reliably identify 82°C; use a thermometer rather than guessing for this formulation.
- An immersion blender removes powder lumps efficiently. Without one, add the dry mixture in small increments while whisking vigorously, then whisk in the whey at the stated cooling temperature until no lumps remain.
- Adding whey after the base cools reduces its exposure to high cooking temperatures, which may otherwise increase aggregation or graininess. Keep the later additions and utensils clean because the mixture is not reheated after adding whey.
- Rapid cooling and refrigerated maturation support safe handling and allow the dairy powders and xanthan to hydrate. Freeze for at least 24 hours rather than relying on the supplied 18-hour minimum; use any longer freezing time required by the machine's manual.
- The 436.9 g base mass is not a volume guarantee: pint sizes and maximum-fill markings vary. Confirm that both the base and subsequent brownie addition comply with the specific Creami model's instructions.
- Complete any necessary Re-Spin before adding brownie. Processing the inclusions again may break them down further, so temper separately frozen finished portions rather than assuming a re-spin will preserve the original pieces.
- Nutrition varies with the cocoa, chocolate whey, cream and brownie yield. The ingredient weights total 496.9 g, so the earlier 258 g portion estimate is corrected to approximately 248 g before cooking losses.
