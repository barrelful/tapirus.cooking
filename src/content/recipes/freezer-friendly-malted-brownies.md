Dense malted chocolate brownies cut into small frozen ice-cream inclusions.\
The supplied butter, oil, sugar and egg formulation is retained without lower-calorie substitutions.\
Designed for individually packed 60 g portions for Ninja Creami mix-ins.

![Illustrative chocolate brownie with nuts on a foil-lined tray; nuts are not included in this recipe](/pix/freezer-friendly-malted-brownies.webp)

- ⏲️ Prep time: ~25 min active, plus ~2 hours cooling, at least 4 hours chilling and ~2 hours freezing
- 🍳 Cook time: ~22–28 min
- 🍽️ Servings: approximately 11 portions (60 g each), plus a small remainder; one 20 cm square batch
- Freezer-friendly: individually packed brownie cubes

## Ingredients

**Brownie batter**
- 100 g 72% dark chocolate, finely chopped
- 37 g cocoa powder
- 56 g boiling water
- 99 g unsalted butter, diced
- 56 g rapeseed oil
- 1.5 g food-grade GMS (glyceryl monostearate), optional
- 100 g brown sugar
- 96 g granulated sugar
- 15 g mild honey
- 1 large egg (approximately 50 g without shell)
- 2 egg yolks (approximately 34 g combined)
- 6 g vanilla extract
- 100 g plain flour
- 18 g Ovaltine Original
- 2 g fine salt (adjust to taste only in a subsequent batch; use 2 g for the first batch)

## Directions

**Equipment and preparation**
1. Gather a 20 cm square metal tin, baking parchment, kitchen scales, two heatproof mixing bowls, a whisk, a spatula, a knife, a chopping board, a freezer tray and airtight containers or freezer bags.
2. Set out a saucepan for a water bath; use a 0.1 g-resolution scale if including GMS, a probe thermometer for temperature checks and a wire rack for cooling if available.
3. Heat the oven to **175°C conventional / 160°C fan**.
4. Line the tin with baking parchment, leaving an overhang on two sides for lifting.
5. Weigh all ingredients before mixing; keep the whole egg and yolks together in the second bowl.

**Cocoa and fat phase**
1. Whisk **37 g cocoa powder with 56 g boiling water** in the first heatproof bowl until smooth.
2. Add the chocolate, butter and rapeseed oil to the cocoa paste; add the GMS if using.
3. Put 500 mL water into a suitably sized saucepan and bring it to a gentle simmer; position the bowl above the water without letting its base touch the water.
4. Stir over the gentle water bath until the chocolate and butter melt into a smooth mixture; if using GMS, follow the supplier's melting instructions and stir until it is fully dispersed. Avoid boiling the mixture.
5. Remove the bowl from the heat and let the mixture cool to **below 50°C**, stirring occasionally, before combining it with the eggs.

**Mixing and baking**
1. Add both sugars, honey and vanilla to the egg and yolks in the second bowl.
2. Whisk until smooth and slightly thickened; avoid whipping the mixture into a foam.
3. Pour the warm chocolate mixture into the egg mixture gradually while stirring until uniform.
4. Combine the flour, Ovaltine and salt in a separate dry bowl.
5. Fold the dry mixture into the chocolate mixture with a spatula; stop as soon as no dry flour remains.
6. Scrape the batter into the lined tin and level the surface.
7. Bake for **22–28 minutes**, checking from 22 minutes; extend baking if necessary until the edges are fully set and the centre is set but still moist.
8. Check the centre with a skewer: require thick fudgy crumbs rather than liquid batter; if using a probe, aim for approximately **88–92°C** at the centre without touching the tin.

**Cooling, cutting and freezing**
1. Cool the brownie in its tin on a rack until it reaches room temperature, normally 1–2 hours.
2. Cover and refrigerate for **at least 4 hours or overnight**, until firm enough to cut cleanly.
3. Lift the slab out using the parchment and weigh it without the paper; record the finished batch mass.
4. Cut into approximately **8–10 mm cubes**, wiping the knife as needed to prevent sticking.
5. Spread the cubes in a single layer on a parchment-lined tray and freeze until firm, approximately 2 hours.
6. Weigh the frozen cubes into **60 g packs**, seal airtight and label with the preparation date; pack any remainder separately with its weight.
7. For each Creami pint, process the ice-cream base first, then add one 60 g pack of frozen cubes and use the model's mix-in procedure; respect its fill limit and inclusion guidance.

## Portioning & Storage

**Per portion (mix-in):**
- 60 g finished brownie, cut into 8–10 mm cubes, for one Creami pint.
- Ice cream is not included in this portion definition or its nutrition.
- Expected cooled yield: approximately 700 g, giving 11 full 60 g portions and approximately 40 g remainder. This is an estimate, not a tested yield.
- Calculate the actual full portion count as the finished batch mass in grams divided by 60, rounded down; retain the remaining grams separately.

**Storage**
- Refrigerator: keep covered at 4°C or below for up to 4 days from baking.
- Freezer: keep individual 60 g packs airtight at −18°C or below for up to 3 months for best quality; remove excess air to reduce freezer burn.
- Mix-in use: add directly from frozen after the ice-cream base has been processed; do not refreeze a partly thawed pack repeatedly.
- Standalone use: thaw a sealed portion overnight in the refrigerator and use within 2 days, within the total 4-day refrigerated allowance. Reheating is unnecessary for mix-ins; for a warm standalone portion, microwave gently in 10-second intervals until just warm.

## Notes

- The ingredient formulation is preserved at the author's request. This is a rich brownie inclusion, not a high-protein or reduced-calorie recipe; the 60 g pack controls the amount added to each pint.
- Blooming the cocoa reduces dry lumps and develops chocolate flavour. Rapeseed oil and sugars may help limit frozen firmness, but the cubes will still become firmer when frozen; frozen texture has not been bench-tested.
- GMS is optional: it supports fat dispersion but its effectiveness depends on the product and melting conditions. If using it, use 1.5 g food-grade glyceryl monostearate suitable for baking, not an unspecified emulsifier blend. To omit it, leave it out without replacing it or changing the other quantities; stir the fat phase thoroughly and combine it gradually with the egg mixture. Eggs provide emulsification without added GMS, although texture and frozen stability may differ; a direct comparison has not been tested. The nutrition estimate includes GMS; omitting it reduces batch energy by approximately 14 kcal and fat by 1.5 g.
- A 0.1 g-resolution scale makes the optional GMS measurement repeatable. Without one, use a supplier-labelled pre-weighed 1.5 g sachet or omit the GMS; a teaspoon or a whole-gram scale is not a reliable substitute for this quantity. Ordinary kitchen scales are sufficient for the other ingredients and 60 g packs.
- A double boiler gives gentle, even heating. Without a dedicated unit, use a heatproof bowl over a saucepan as directed; without that setup, use a microwave-safe bowl in short 10–15 second bursts, stirring thoroughly between bursts until smooth.
- A probe thermometer makes cooling and doneness checks easier. Without one, let the melted mixture become only mildly warm before adding it to the eggs, and use both the set-centre and fudgy-crumb tests when baking; wet, flowing batter requires more baking.
- A wire rack speeds cooling beneath the tin. Without one, cool on a heatproof trivet with space around the tin; do not seal the brownie while hot.
- A metal tin gives more predictable baking than glass or ceramic. If only a 20 cm square glass or ceramic dish is available, start at 160°C conventional / 145°C fan and allow extra time, using the same doneness checks rather than the metal-tin timing.
- A freezer tray prevents clumping. Without one, use a parchment-lined freezer-safe plate or shallow container, keeping cubes in one layer until frozen, then transfer into individual packs.
- Chilling before cutting firms the fat phase and reduces smearing. Minimal flour folding deliberately follows this adaptation rather than the reference video's extended mixing for a chewier texture.
- The reference uses a different chocolate formulation and milk-chocolate pieces. This version preserves the supplied 72% chocolate, Ovaltine, honey, GMS and egg/yolk formulation without adding those pieces.
- Ovaltine Original formulations vary by market; use the product named in the ingredient list and check its allergen label. No nuts are added to this recipe, although the illustrative photograph shows nuts.
